### 1. RingCentral (RNG) — $4.4B · composite 69

**What they do.** Cloud business-phone and communications: RingEX (calling/messaging/video), the RingCX contact-center suite, and a growing shelf of paid AI agents (AI Receptionist, agentic support). It sells the "replace your desk phone system" pitch to mid-market and enterprise.

**Business model.** Subscription, >90% of revenue, high net retention (>100%). Q2 2026: revenue **$657M (+6% y/y)**, and the headline is cash: **free cash flow $180M (27% margin), FY26 FCF guidance raised to $615–625M**, non-GAAP operating margin ~24%. Paid-AI ARR **doubled y/y** to 13% of ARR. They also just **initiated/raised a dividend** ($0.125/qtr) — a maturity signal.

**Moat.** Moderate and eroding-at-the-edges. Switching costs are real (phone systems are sticky, numbers port painfully), but UCaaS is a brutal neighborhood — Microsoft Teams Phone bundles "free" into every E5 seat, and Zoom, 8x8, and Cisco all compete. RingCentral's edge is being the best *pure-play* independent, but that's a share-of-a-maturing-pie story.

**Growth trajectory.** Decelerated to mid-single-digits; the equity story has flipped from growth to FCF-and-capital-return. AI is the only reacceleration lever, and it's early but genuinely doubling.

**Cons / risks.** ~5–6% growth against Microsoft's structural bundling threat; still carries meaningful debt from its growth years; the stock has been a multi-year underperformer as UCaaS de-rated. This is a "cash cow proving it can defend itself," not a grower.

**Fair take.** Screen loved it for **quality (85) + technicals (78)** — strong FCF conversion and an improving tape — while fundamentals (53) flag the slow growth. A deleveraging FCF machine with an AI option; the bull case is margins and buybacks, not revenue.

### 2. Dropbox (DBX) — $7.0B · composite 68

**What they do.** File sync/share for 18M+ paying users, plus Dropbox Sign (e-sign), DocSend, and the AI bet **Dash** (universal search across a company's scattered content).

**Business model.** Freemium subscription, essentially **flat revenue** (Q1 2026 $629.5M, +0.8% y/y; Q2 guide implies <1% growth ex-divestiture). The whole story is capital return: **~$367M of buybacks in Q1** (14.3M shares), ~$800M authorization remaining, unlevered FCF **$236M/quarter**. ARPU ~$139.

**Moat.** Modest — brand, cross-platform neutrality, huge installed base, but competes with storage bundled free into Microsoft 365, Google, iCloud. Inertia more than lock-in.

**Growth trajectory.** Flat, and has been for years. Paying users have wobbled. Dash is the first genuinely new demand vector but remains unproven as revenue.

**Cons / risks.** The buyback engine is **debt-funded** — stockholders' equity is negative, so if FCF slips the leverage bites. Secular-relevance question vs. bundled suites never goes away; Dash faces Microsoft Copilot and Glean.

**Fair take.** Same profile as it earned last week — **quality 85** rewarding cash conversion and aggressive share shrink. A deep-value cannibal: cheap, huge FCF, no growth, some leverage. A return-of-capital holding, not a compounder.

### 3. Gen Digital (GEN) — $16.1B · composite 67

**What they do.** Consumer cyber-safety conglomerate — **Norton, Avast, LifeLock, Avira, CCleaner** — and, since 2025, **MoneyLion**, pushing them into consumer fintech (the "financial + digital safety" bundle). Largest name in this week's top 5.

**Business model.** Subscription, ~79M paying customers. FY2026 (ended ~March): **revenue $5.0B, +27% y/y** — but that growth is **mostly the MoneyLion acquisition**, not organic; organic is low-to-mid single digits. Non-GAAP EPS $2.56 (+15%), **FCF $1.5B (>30% of revenue)**. FY27 guide: 8–10% revenue, mid-teens EPS.

**Moat.** Decent in its core: consumer security is a habit/renewal business with strong brand recall and auto-renew inertia (Norton/LifeLock are household names). The MoneyLion fintech extension is unproven and adds execution + credit risk.

**Growth trajectory.** Optically excellent (+27%) but acquisition-fueled; the honest number is organic mid-single-digits plus M&A. The bet is cross-selling fintech into the security base.

**Cons / risks.** The 27% headline **overstates underlying momentum** — read it as a roll-up. Consumer cyber is mature and competitive (McAfee, Microsoft Defender is "good enough" for many); MoneyLion integration and consumer-credit exposure are new risk vectors; carries acquisition debt.

**Fair take.** Screen ranked it on **fundamentals (74)** — the strongest in the group, reflecting real profitability and FCF — with only middling quality (53). A cash-generative consumer-software roll-up; underwrite the *organic* growth and the fintech bet, not the M&A-inflated headline.

### 4. Progress Software (PRGS) — $1.6B · composite 65

**What they do.** A deliberately unglamorous **"acquire mature infrastructure software and milk it"** operator: OpenEdge, MOVEit (the file-transfer product from the 2023 breach), WhatsUp Gold, Chef, MarkLogic, and now **ShareFile** (secure document collaboration, ~$240M ARR).

**Business model.** The model *is* the strategy: buy profitable software, cut costs, harvest cash, repeat. Q2 2026: revenue **$253.5M (+7% y/y, acquisition-driven)**, ARR $868M (+2% organic), **net retention only 100%**, non-GAAP EPS $1.62 (+16%), FCF guide $271–283M.

**Moat.** Per-product switching costs are high (this software is embedded and boring-critical), but there's no growth moat — it's a **financial** strategy, not a franchise. Value comes from disciplined capital allocation, not expansion.

**Growth trajectory.** ~flat organically (ARR +2%, NRR 100%); all reported growth is M&A. That's by design.

**Cons / risks.** **Leverage is the watch item** — ~$1.29B total debt, net leverage ~2.9x, because debt funds the acquisitions. The whole thesis depends on management keep finding accretive deals at sane prices and not fumbling integration; MOVEit also carries lingering breach-reputation/security-liability tail risk.

**Fair take.** Screen liked **quality (84) + technicals (71)**; fundamentals (52) flag the no-organic-growth reality. A well-run cash-compounder-by-acquisition for investors who trust the capital allocation — not a growth story, and the leverage means it's not risk-free either.

### 5. Qualys (QLYS) — $4.9B · composite 64

**What they do.** Cloud vulnerability management and its newer Enterprise TruRisk Management (ETM) exposure-management suite. (Covered in depth last week — it was #1 then, #5 now.)

**Business model.** Elite-margin subscription SaaS: **~83% gross margin, mid-40s% EBITDA margin, low-40s% FCF margin**. Q1 2026 revenue +10%; Q2 guide $177.5–179.5M (+8–9%); FY26 raised. A cash machine.

**Moat.** Real switching costs (VM data + compliance workflows embed into security ops and audit), 25-year brand — but a *feature-scale* moat converging with the platforms (CrowdStrike, Wiz, Tenable, Palo Alto all sell exposure management).

**Growth trajectory.** Decelerated to high-single-digits; mature, defensible, cash-gushing — not a grower.

**Cons / risks.** Single-product-line concentration; platform convergence pressure; **slipped from #1 to #5** as its quality score dropped to 44 this week (weaker on the accrual/dilution read than the group). Insiders keep selling (below).

**Fair take.** Screen ranked it on **fundamentals (66)** and a strong tape (70); quality (44) is the drag. Durable FCF and the ETM option, priced like the mature asset it is.
